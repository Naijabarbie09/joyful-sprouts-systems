# JSO Five-Year Plan, version 0 (2026-11-17)

Goal set by the owner: a multi-million-dollar business within five years (by November 2031).

This is a first frame, not a forecast. JSO has no sales history yet, and no reliable public data exists on how long comparable brands took to reach $1M (see `docs/competitor-benchmarks.md`). Revenue markers below are **targets worked backwards from the goal**. They get replaced with JSO's own numbers as soon as those exist.

## What the goal requires, in plain arithmetic

- Stock on hand today is worth $24,720 at full price. Selling every unit is about 1% of a $2M year.
- A $2M year at a $52 average order (the Everyday Pair price, used as a placeholder) is about 38,500 orders.
- At a 1.6% store conversion rate (apparel benchmark, vendor-blog quality) that is about 2.4 million sessions a year, roughly 200,000 a month. JSO had 1,306 sessions in the last 90 days.
- Average Meta cost per purchase is about $39, more than either product's price. Buying single-item first orders with ads loses money. Growth has to come from email, community, gifting and registry, repeat purchase, bundles and retail partners.

So the path is: more products per customer, more channels than the website alone, and proof of demand before each new commitment of cash.

## Stage gates

A stage ends when its exit test is met, not when the calendar says so.

| Stage | Revenue marker (target, not forecast) | What has to be true to exit |
|---|---|---|
| **0. Legal and ready to sell** (now) | $0 | Certificate and test reports in hand; labels checked; sleeper sizing resolved; stock counted; insurance bound; tax letters handled; Kickstarter backers made whole |
| **1. First 100 customers** (target: by spring 2027) | About $5K to $8K | 100 paid orders from people who are not friends and family; 20 or more reviews; known cost per order; conversion measured on at least 3,000 sessions; email list above 1,000 |
| **2. Sell through Drop 1 and prove repeat** (target: within 2027) | About $25K cumulative | 80% of Drop 1 sold at an average discount under 15%; repeat rate measured; size curve known; contribution margin positive after postage and packaging |
| **3. A real line** (2028) | $100K to $150K a year | Drop 2 funded from Drop 1 cash plus approved capital; 8 to 12 products including a gift set; Babylist listing; first 10 to 20 boutique accounts; one channel besides the website producing 25% of revenue |
| **4. Scale what works** (2029 to 2030) | $400K to $1M a year | Email and repeat customers above 35% of revenue; paid acquisition profitable on first-order margin or a measured 12-month value; a fulfilment partner; first part-time hires |
| **5. Multi-million** (2031) | $2M or more a year | Several hundred wholesale doors or a national retail partner, plus DTC; seasonal drop calendar; working-capital line in place |

Each step from stage 3 on implies growing 2.5 to 4 times a year. That is aggressive. More than 90% of US e-commerce companies stay under $1M (McKinsey). The plan should be judged at each gate, and the owner should expect the PM agent to say so if a gate is being missed.

## Strategy choices this plan assumes (open to challenge by data)

1. **Do not compete on price.** Carter's and Burt's Bees sell GOTS organic bodysuits at about $6 each. JSO's lane is the gift-quality, sensitive-skin, founder-led brand.
2. **Lead with the sleeper and with bundles.** The $32 sleeper is priced under its boutique peers; the $24 single bodysuit is at the top of its range. Bundles raise order value enough to cover shipping.
3. **Gifting and registry early.** Babylist is used by a large share of expecting families; the organic-cotton peers all sell there or through Maisonette and Target.com.
4. **Community before ads.** The only documented fast-growth peer did no paid ads in year one.
5. **Wholesale once paperwork is clean.** Boutiques and marketplaces will ask for the certificate.
6. **No new inventory until Drop 1 proves demand.** That includes the 800-swaddle minimum.
