Lessons the evidence supports:
- The one documented fast path (Little Sleepies) used a fabric hook, limited print drops and a Facebook community, with no paid ads in year one. JSO has the founder story and community start, not the fabric hook or prints.
- Kyte Baby shares JSO's origin story (eczema) and still took years to take off.
- The organic-cotton brands (Colored Organics, Pehr, Primary, Monica + Andy) all lean on retail partners: Target.com, Maisonette, Babylist, boutiques.
- Years-to-$1M could not be verified for any brand.

## 3. The giant in the category

Carter's, fiscal 2025 ([release](https://ir.carters.com/node/19926), [Q4 exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1060822/000106082226000015/ex991cri-q42025.htm)): net sales $2.898B; US retail 50.6%, US wholesale 34.5%, international 14.9%; gross margin 45.4%, down 260 basis points, with about $40M of tariff cost in Q4; operating margin 5.0% vs 9.0% the year before; about 150 store closures announced Oct 2025 ([CoStar](https://www.costar.com/article/1817997516/walloped-by-tariffs-childrens-apparel-chain-carters-to-close-150-stores)). Share claims conflict by age band: 33% of ages 0 to 12 months ([Carter's ESG](https://esg.carters.com/2025-about-carters)) vs 21% of ages 0 to 2 ([The Hustle](https://thehustle.co/news/from-what-babies-wear-to-who-they-are-carters-big-shift)).

Takeaway: the market leader sells GOTS organic bodysuits at about $6 each and is losing margin. JSO cannot win on price and should not try.

## 6. Rules that apply to JSO (confirm each with the regulator page or a professional)

- **Children's Product Certificate** based on third-party testing is required ([CPSC](https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Testing-Certification/Childrens-Product-Certificate/)). The importer issues it. Certificate e-filing for imports became mandatory from Jul 8, 2026 ([FedEx notice](https://www.fedex.com/content/dam/fedex/us-united-states/International/CPSC_to_implement_mandatory_electronic_filing_requirements_in_ACE_on_July_8_2026.pdf)).
- **Small batch manufacturer registration** exists and gives some testing relief; which tests it relieves for apparel is NOT VERIFIED ([CPSC](https://www.cpsc.gov/smallbatch)). Tracking labels are still required.
- **Sleepwear.** Sizes above 9 months fall under 16 CFR 1615. Garments sized 9 months or smaller, and tight-fitting garments, are exempt from 1615/1616 and tested under 16 CFR 1610 instead ([CPSC](https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Business-Education/Business-Guidance/Childrens-Sleepwear-Regulations)). JSO's 6-12M and 12-18M sleepers must meet the tight-fitting dimensions with the required label and hangtag, or pass flame testing.
- **FTC labels:** fibre content, country of origin, manufacturer identity, care ([FTC](https://business.ftc.gov/documents/bus21-threading-your-way-through-labeling-requirements-under-textile-and-wool-acts)).
- **"Organic" and GOTS:** a GOTS label needs the whole chain and final product certified and must show the label grade, certifier and licence number ([OTA](https://ota.com/sites/default/files/docs/BestLabelingPractices_Textiles_05152019_Links_updated.pdf), [USDA](https://www.ams.usda.gov/sites/default/files/media/NOP-PM-11-14-LabelingofTextiles.pdf)).
- **Tariffs on India:** changed three times in 2026; secondary sources put a cotton knit garment at about 26.5% all-in since Jul 24, 2026 ([Intoglo](https://www.intoglo.com/blog/us-section-301-tariff-on-india-2026-rate-impact-exemptions)). Confirm with a customs broker before the next order.
