# Organic Cotton Sleeper (Drop 1): compliance status and plan

Prepared by the PM agent, 2026-11-17. Based on the tech pack (V3-CORRECTED), the UPS CPSC form dated 2026-10-29, the live product page, and CPSC guidance read through search extracts. Not legal advice. No sleeper garment, label, zipper or hangtag has been inspected yet.

## Why the sleeper is harder than the bodysuit

The store sells it "for bedtime, naps". That makes it children's sleepwear, and for children 3 and under it is also a "child care article". Two extra sets of rules follow: the sleepwear flammability standard (16 CFR Part 1615) and the phthalates limits (16 CFR Part 1307).

## By size

| Size | Sleepwear rule | Status |
|---|---|---|
| NB, 0-3M, 3-6M | Exempt as "infant garments": sized 9 months or smaller, one-piece length no more than 25.75 in, labelled with size in months. Tech pack lengths are 19.5 to 21.5 in. Falls back to 16 CFR 1610, where the fabric weight exemption applies. | Likely fine. Check that the printed label states the size in months ("NB" alone may not). |
| 6-12M | Not an infant garment (size runs above 9 months). Must be "tight-fitting" or pass flame testing. | Open |
| 12-18M | Same | Open, higher risk |

## What "tight-fitting" requires (6-12M and 12-18M)

1. **Every measurement at or under the maximum for the size.** Confirmed maximums for 9-12 months (2013 CFR edition; check the current eCFR): chest 48.3 cm, waist 48.3 cm, seat 48.3 cm, upper arm 14.3 cm, thigh 26.7 cm, wrist 10.5 cm, ankle 13 cm. Each is measured flat and doubled. The 12-18 months row is NOT VERIFIED.
   - Tech pack chest for 9-12M is 9.5 in flat = 19 in = 48.3 cm, exactly at the limit, with a +/- 0.25 in tolerance.
   - Tech pack chest for 12-18M is 10.0 in flat = 20 in = 50.8 cm.
   - The tech pack gives no waist, seat, upper arm, thigh, wrist or ankle measurements.
2. **Permanent neck label** reading "WEAR SNUG-FITTING, NOT FLAME RESISTANT" directly below the size.
3. **Hangtag** with the required snug-fit safety message (exact wording and format to be taken from 16 CFR 1615.1(o)).

Neither label is in the tech pack. If the produced sleepers match the bodysuit's label, they do not carry them.

## Parts that need their own evidence (all sizes)

| Part | Rule | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| Zipper slider, pull, stops | Lead content; lead in paint if the pull is coated | The stainless steel exemption on the UPS form is unlikely to fit a zipper. Get the zipper maker's compliance certificate (YKK and similar publish them). |
| Silicone foot grips (6-12M, 12-18M) | Lead content; phthalates | Supplier component test report or certificate |
| Printed neck label ink | Lead content; phthalates | Supplier statement or test report |
| Fabric, thread, embroidery | Lead (textile determination); flammability (weight exemption) | Fabric weight in writing |

A registered CPSC small batch manufacturer may be able to rely on supplier component certificates instead of its own third-party tests for some of these. Which rules qualify is NOT VERIFIED and must be read from CPSC's list.

## Recommended plan

1. **NB to 3-6M:** same path as the bodysuit, plus the zipper certificate and label ink statement. Then issue a certificate.
2. **6-12M and 12-18M: hold.** Measure three units of each size and colour against the full table. Then:
   - Within limits: add the neck statement and hangtag, gather the grip and zipper evidence, issue a certificate.
   - Over limits: they cannot be sold as sleepwear as they are. Untreated cotton is not expected to pass the sleepwear flame test. Options are alteration, holding the stock, or a write-off.
3. **Do not relabel the larger sizes as a daytime "playsuit" to avoid the rule.** CPSC has said in writing it will act against garments marketed to get around the sleepwear standard, and a footed zip one-piece sold for bedtime is a clear case.
4. Bundles that include a 6-12M or 12-18M sleeper (Everyday Pair and Starter Set in sizes 6-9M, 9-12M, 12-18M) are affected by the hold.
5. Correct the UPS blanket form for the sleeper once the zipper material is known.

## Exposure

About 170 of the 421 sleepers are in the two larger sizes (Sage is 43 + 43 in Shopify; Oat assumed similar, not re-counted), roughly $5,400 at full price.

### Label fix spec (draft, confirm wording and format in 16 CFR 1615.1(o)(10) and (11))

| Item | Requirement | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Neck statement | "WEAR SNUG-FITTING, NOT FLAME RESISTANT", capitals, at the centre back neck directly below the size | Wording and placement from the CPSC FAQ. Minimum 5 point sans serif is from a secondary source. |
| Hangtag text | "For child's safety, garment should fit snugly. This garment is not flame resistant. Loose-fitting garment is more likely to catch fire." | Wording from the eCFR extract |
| Hangtag format | Yellow tag, black text, Arial or Helvetica | Colour from the CPSC FAQ; font from the Federal Register correction. Tag size and type size NOT VERIFIED (recalled as 1.5 x 6.25 in and 18 point). |
| Packaging | If sold in a package, the package carries the same hangtag wording | eCFR extract |

Applies to every 6-12M and 12-18M sleeper before it is sold, gifted or shipped. The neck statement has to be permanent (heat-pressed print or sewn label).

Keep a measurement record: date, size, colour, unit count, each of the seven measurements, and the limit used.
